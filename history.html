History

More than a century of technical excellence and legendary cars.

Discover The Pure Beauty

TYPE 13, 22, 23

BUGATTI's first great triumph at the Grand Prix de la Sarthe in Le Mans came in 1920 with the Type 13 Brescia. With a relatively small but powerful four-cylinder engine, 16 valves and a lightweight construction, the Type 13 was able to compete against much larger and heavier cars. The 1920s and 1930s became Bugatti's Golden Age. The Type 13 established the company's reputation in motorsport, while the Type 35 later became one of the most successful Grand Prix racing cars in automotive history.

TYPE 3, 8, 54

Automobiles Ettore Bugatti was founded in 1909 in Molsheim, Alsace, by Italian-born automobile designer and engineer Ettore Bugatti. Ettore grew up in a creative family. His father, Carlo Bugatti, was a famous furniture and jewellery designer. Ettore combined engineering with artistic design and developed automobiles that became famous for their performance, lightweight construction and elegant appearance. Before creating his own automobile company, Ettore worked with several manufacturers and developed many early automotive designs.

TYPE 17, 2, 62

Bugatti became one of the world's most respected manufacturers of high-performance automobiles. The company became famous for combining advanced engineering, racing performance and beautiful design. Some of its most legendary automobiles include the Type 35 Grand Prix car, the Type 41 Royale, the Type 57 Atlantic and the Type 55 sports car. The death of Ettore Bugatti in 1947 was a major turning point for the company. His son Jean Bugatti had already died in 1939, leaving the company without its expected successor.

TYPE 1, 8, 73

The 1920s and 1930s represented the Golden Age of Bugatti. During this period, the company became famous both on the racing circuit and among luxury automobile customers. The Type 35 became one of the most successful racing cars ever created. Bugatti also introduced extraordinary luxury automobiles such as the Type 41 Royale with its enormous engine and the Type 57 Atlantic, which became one of the most celebrated automotive designs in history. These cars represented Ettore Bugatti's belief that automobiles should combine engineering, speed and art.
